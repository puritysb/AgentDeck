# 2026-10-08 — Pixoo64 "Tide": a scene drawn for the panel, uploaded rarely

After the baked loop (#476) was enabled on the owner's Pixoo64, the owner reported that the panel kept showing its loading hourglass and suggested replacing the animation with one made for the device, keeping the usage strip. The loop path re-baked and re-uploaded 12–14 frames every 10–13 s, and a device that shows a hourglass during every ingest shows it every 10–13 s. The source also showed that the aquarium itself suits an LED matrix badly: the agent mark was the official 24×24 mask zoomed to about 58 px and filled the panel, and the water was a low-contrast gradient that turned to mush.

**Scene.** `pixoo-tide.ts` draws rows 0–49 (or 0–56 / 0–63 with one / no usage provider) from flat depth bands dithered at the seams, a travelling waterline, kelp, sand and ambient fish, with the official masks at native size. A working mark bobs and a light band sweeps it once per loop; a needs-you mark has the amber dotted ring, the only pulse on the panel. The HUD below is the existing `drawUsageHUD`, untouched. Marks are chosen loudest first (awaiting, processing, idle), at most four, with a `+N` count; an unknown `agentType` draws nothing, and Codex rows fold by project as in the aquarium.

**Loop.** Six frames at 500 ms. Every animated value is a function of `tick / 6` and there is no `Math.random`, so the loop closes and the output can be used as identity. The test measures the step from the last frame back to the first against every other step; breaking a wave period or a bubble rise made it fail before the change was reverted.

**What the hourglass is, measured.** Timed uploads against the panel with no daemon running: ~0.17 s for one frame, 1.05 s for four, 3.1–3.2 s for twelve (about 0.27 s per frame). The owner watched it: the hourglass appears when the **content changes** (every creature change, and when sessions went from one to three), not during the verification queries (`Channel/GetIndex`, `Draw/GetHttpGifId`) or a brightness set. The owner could not say from which frame count it starts, so the loop was shortened from 12 to 8 frames (3.2 s → 2.1 s of ingest), and then to 6 (1.6 s) when the owner found the hourglass still slightly too long, rather than to a guessed threshold.

**Upload policy.** The first design re-baked on any state-hash change and the owner still saw the hourglass: on a busy desk the hash (every session's state, the reset countdown's minute) changes every few seconds, so measurement showed an upload every 6–9 s. The policy now keys on what the panel would show (`tideSignature`: marks, states, CI cue vs. the usage strip): a scene change at most every 15 s, a usage-only change at most every 5 min, otherwise a 30 s verification by two tiny queries. Measured over five minutes on a busy desk: 3 uploads (two at startup, one when sessions went from one to three) against ~25–30 for the old loop. The remaining hourglass is the ingest of a real scene change.

**Two things measured on the way.** The device's PicID counter does not equal the uploaded ID (215 → 217 after one single-frame upload, → 218 after a four-frame one; after a reset, pushing ID 1 leaves it at 2), so a verification by equality called every healthy panel stale and re-uploaded forever; it is `>=` now (a reboot lowers the counter, a button press changes the channel). And after a four-frame upload followed by single-frame uploads the owner saw wrong colours and a tangled picture until the counter was reset and a clean frame sent. Cause not proven (the leading suspect is leftover frames of the previous animation being mixed with the new one), so tide never falls back to single frames: a failed upload backs tide off for 60 s.

**Not done.** The Swift daemon is still single-frame and has no tide scene, so a roaming daemon shows the aquarium. Port the scene to `PixooRenderer.swift` only once the owner has accepted the look. Device reboot and failed-upload recovery are covered by unit tests of the decision, not yet exercised on the panel.
